## 2. Results established (all exact, no simulation)

### The λ law — one quantity behind everything

For independent links the expected `L`-cycle count is `tr(P^L)/2L`. For a
`K`-block fit this collapses onto a `K×K` transfer matrix

    T_rs = ω_rs · sqrt(Q_r Q_s)      (degree-corrected, Q_r = Σ_{i∈r} k_i²)
    T_rs = p_rs · sqrt(n_r n_s)      (plain)

so the **entire motif spectrum is generated by one small matrix** and is a smooth
geometric profile `λ^L/2L`. A two-variable model cannot place a feature at any
particular length. With `λ ≈ ⟨k²⟩/⟨k⟩`:

    λ ~ n^{(3-γ)/(γ-1)}   (γ < 3)        λ ~ ln n   (γ = 3)
    deficit at length L ≈ n / λ^L        crossover at L* = (γ-1)/(3-γ)

Undercounts while `λ^L < n`, overcounts after.

### Numbers

| | pseudofractal (γ≈2.585) | diamond (γ=3) |
|---|---|---|
| rule arity | 3 (triangles) | 4 (four-cycles) |
| true motif count | exactly `n-2` triangles | exactly `4^{t-1}` four-cycles |
| plain SBM | saturates at **21.3** | saturates at **81/4 = 20.25** |
| plain deficit | `~n` | `~n` |
| degree-corrected | `(64/27)^t` | `(t+1)^4/4`, since `Q/S = t+1` exactly |
| DC deficit | `n^{0.214}` | `n/(ln n)^4` |

Two independent derivations of the λ exponent agree to 4 dp:
`(3-γ)/(γ-1) = 0.2619` and `ln(4/3)/ln 3 = 0.2619`.
Predicted crossover `L* = 3.82` matches the observed flip between L=3 and L=4.

### The spectrum result (the strongest one)

Diamond at `t=5`, counted exactly: **0** three-cycles, **256** four-cycles,
**0** at five, six and seven, **1024** eight-cycles. Support is `L = 4, 8, 16, …`
and empty elsewhere. Both fitted models put positive mass at *every* length
(plain: 8.3 triangles, 42 five-cycles; DC: 69, 1560).

**The failure is of support, not magnitude.** No choice of blocks or degrees moves
a smooth geometric profile onto a sparse set of lengths. This is the real argument
for the conjecture: a higher-order object buys not a bigger number but the ability
to have a shape.

Gotcha found by enumeration: the diamond's 8-cycles number `4^t`, **not** `4^{t-2}`
— each of the four positions on a parent 4-cycle offers two parallel paths, so
`2^4` routes per parent. The closed-form guess was wrong by 16×. Enumerate before
trusting a recursion here.

## 3. PENDING — the experiment this session could not run

**Test the crossover length `L*` on real networks.** This is the chapter's
falsifiable prediction and the natural next step: fit a block model to a scale-free
network and find where its motif counts cross over from under- to over-counting.

There are **two forms of the prediction, and they are separately falsifiable.**
Do not conflate them — the second is the headline claim and the stronger test.

    (a) operational:  L* = ln n / ln λ        with λ measured directly
    (b) headline:     L* = (γ-1)/(3-γ)        from the degree exponent alone

(b) follows from (a) only through `λ ~ n^{(3-γ)/(γ-1)}`, which is itself an
assumption — it presumes a clean power law with the natural cutoff and no
structural cutoff, and it is the step most likely to break on real data. So (b)
tests the transfer-matrix theory **and** the λ–γ scaling relation at once, and can
fail while (a) still holds.

Design consequence: **always measure `λ = ⟨k²⟩/⟨k⟩` directly** rather than
inferring it from a fitted `γ`, and test the λ–γ relation as its own step by
regressing `ln λ` on `ln n` within families of similar `γ`. Report which of the
two forms survives; they are different results.

### Why it stalled here

- **no graph-tool** in this environment (networkx only) — cannot fit SBMs;
- **no topology in the repo** — `figs/data/*.csv` are summary statistics only
  (n, E, mean_k, mean_c, B_mean …), no edge lists.

So it needs an environment with graph-tool *and* the Netzschleuder networks.

### Specification

1. Take real networks spanning a range of `γ` (Netzschleuder; the ~100 used for
   `figs/data/community_communities_vs_n.csv` are the natural set). Span matters:
   `L*` moves from ~2 to ∞ as `γ → 3`, so a narrow `γ` range tests nothing.
2. Fit the degree-corrected SBM — use `minimize_nested_blockmodel_dl`, **not** the
   flat `minimize_blockmodel_dl` (see §4 below).
3. Compute `λ` = leading eigenvalue of the fitted model's transfer matrix
   `T_rs = ω_rs sqrt(Q_r Q_s)`. For the rank-one baseline `λ = ⟨k²⟩/⟨k⟩` exactly,
   needing no fit at all.
4. Fit `γ` separately (MLE on the tail, not a log-log regression) — needed for
   form (b) and for the λ–γ relation, and kept independent of `λ`.
5. Count true cycles `L = 3…8`.
6. Compare `true_L` against `λ^L / 2L`; locate the crossover; compare with both
   `ln n / ln λ` and `(γ-1)/(3-γ)`.

Practical range check before spending compute: the crossover is only *visible* if
`λ^3 < n < λ^8`, i.e. `n^{1/8} < λ < n^{1/3}`. Outside that window every countable
length sits on one side of the crossover and only the slope test (below) has
content. Compute `λ` and `n` first and bin the networks accordingly.

### What confirms or refutes

The sharp test does **not** depend on where the crossover happens to fall:

    ln(true_L / predicted_L)  should be LINEAR IN L with slope exactly  -ln λ

with zero crossing at `L* = ln n / ln λ`. A slope significantly different from
`-ln λ` refutes the transfer-matrix theory.

Three outcomes, three different papers:

| slope = -ln λ ? | crossover at `(γ-1)/(3-γ)` ? | reading |
|---|---|---|
| yes | yes | both claims hold — the headline result |
| yes | no | transfer-matrix theory holds; the λ–γ scaling fails on real degree sequences (cutoffs). Report `L* = ln n/ln λ` as the correct form |
| no | — | the theory is wrong; the lattices were special. Report that too |

**Control (important):** rewire preserving every degree (`double_edge_swap`, or
graph-tool's `random_rewire`). There the rank-one model *is* the truth, so the
ratio should sit near 1 at all L — slope ≈ 0. This isolates locality from the
degree sequence and is the version that speaks to the book's thesis.

If graph-tool is unavailable but topology is, the rank-one baseline (step 3,
second sentence) needs only degrees, so most of the test runs without any fitting.

### Validated tool — exact cycle counts from traces

Cross-checked against exhaustive DFS enumeration on both lattices (match exact):

    C3 = tr(A³)/6
    C4 = [tr(A⁴) - 2Σd² + 2m] / 8
    C5 = [tr(A⁵) - 5·tr(A³) - 5·Σ_i (d_i - 2)(A³)_ii] / 10

Beyond L=5 use enumeration (`true_cycles` in `figs/motif_spectrum.py`); the L=6
trace formula is long and error-prone. Enumeration cost is `n · ⟨k⟩^{L-1}` and
blows up on hubs — cap the size or the length.

## 4. PENDING — re-run the community census with the nested SBM

Chapter 6's `B ∝ n^0.61` came from `minimize_blockmodel_dl`, the **flat** model,
whose resolution ceiling scales as `√N` (Peixoto, PRX 2014 — the same paper the
chapter cites). The ceiling and the null under test have the same functional form.

The bias is conservative — a ceiling can only pull the fitted exponent *down*, so
`β > 1/2` is measured in spite of the instrument. But these data **cannot certify
an asymptotic exponent above 1/2**, because the estimator cannot report one.
Re-running with `minimize_nested_blockmodel_dl` (ceiling `~n/ln n`) settles it.

If `β` survives, Eq. (7.5) converts it directly into a statement about the
roughness `α` of the limit. This is the single highest-value outstanding
measurement in the chapter.

## 5. Open caveats

- **Bibliography.** 11 of the 13 new entries were written from memory. Verified
  from PDFs to hand: `palla2010` (PNAS 107(17):7640–7645), `backhausz2018`
  (cited as arXiv:1811.00626 to sidestep uncertainty over journal publication).
  Journal/volume/year checked by search for `olhede2014` and `gao2015`.
  **Page numbers and DOIs elsewhere need checking before print.**
- **Hypergraphon pair-indexing.** §7.9 argues the right object has arguments
  indexed by *pairs* (`W(x_ij, x_ik, x_jk)`), not nodes. That is my reading of the
  hypergraph-limit framework (Elek–Szegedy, Tao), not verified in the sources, and
  it is load-bearing for Question 2.
- **Community-size arithmetic.** `B ∝ n^β` means mean community size `n^{1-β}`,
  so `β=0.61` gives communities of `n^{0.39}` — *smaller* than `√n`. The "`n^α`
  with `α ≥ 0.5`" framing corresponds to `β ≤ 0.5`, the fractal case. Worth
  reconciling; it changes what the recursive picture looks like.
- **Chapter 6 overlap.** Its closing calls `β` "super-fractal"; chapter 7 reframes
  the same number as roughness. One of the two should probably give way.
- **Novelty of the RG-as-limit-theory proposal** (§7.8 and the chat around it)
  rests on two web searches, not a literature review. Check "inverse limits" and
  "self-similar graph limits" before building on it.
